WASHINGTON – Graham Platner is suspending his U.S. Senate candidacy in Maine and removing his name from the ballot, he announced Wednesday night, halting a tumultuous campaign that has been haunted from the start by the Marine Corps veteran’s past.

"We believe that for the movement to continue, it can't be me. And for that reason, we are suspending campaign operations," Platner said in a July 8 video message.

Following a steady drumbeat of scandals, Platner’s high-stakes bid for an elusive GOP-held seat in the Pine Tree State imploded this week after a woman he dated years ago publicly accused him of sexual assault — an allegation he denies.

In recent days, prominent Democrats and financial backers quickly pulled their support, all but dooming his candidacy even as he resisted calls to step aside.

"This is incredibly difficult because I know that some will think it's an admission of guilt. And it most certainly is not," Platner said. "We're not doing it because of the allegations. We're doing it because of the structures being taken away from us by those in power."


The political catastrophe for the oyster farmer has major stakes. Whether Democrats can unseat longtime Republican Sen. Susan Collins could determine their ability to take back control of the Senate in November's midterm elections. If they succeed, they could block much of President Donald Trump's second-term legislative agenda.

In an emergency meeting Wednesday night, state party leaders in Maine voted to hold a nominating convention in the coming weeks. They said more details and a specific timeline would be announced "soon."


Under Maine law, a new nominee must be chosen by Monday, July 27, to make it on this fall’s ballot.

Among the potential contenders to take his place is Troy Jackson, the former Democratic president of the Maine state Senate (an exploratory committee filed official paperwork on his behalf). Also in the mix are Nirav Shah, a former state public health official, and Shenna Bellows, the current Maine secretary of state.
